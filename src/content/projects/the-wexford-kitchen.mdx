When the Wexfords called us, the kitchen hadn't been touched since 1987. The room
had good bones — original casing, a south-facing bay — and three layout problems
the previous owners had papered over with peninsulas, microwave hutches, and a
desk nook that nobody used.

We took twelve weeks and four design rounds to get to the right plan. The
banquette alone went through six versions. What you see now: a working kitchen
that doesn't apologize for being in a 1920s Colonial.

## What we kept

- The original casing on the doorways
- The bay window — re-flashed, not replaced
- The wide-plank pine floor (sanded, not stained)

## What we changed

- Removed the wall between kitchen and former butler's pantry
- New rift-sawn white oak cabinetry, soapstone counters, unlacquered brass hardware
- Custom banquette built around the bay with a 24" deep cushion (most banquettes are 18" — that's why they're uncomfortable)
- A working scullery off the main room for the things you don't want on a counter

## What it cost

Six months, mid-six-figures, one slightly-late delivery on the range hood.
Worth every dollar — and we'll say that on the record.
